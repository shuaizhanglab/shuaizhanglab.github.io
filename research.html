Research

We use infrared and terahertz nano-optics to study how quantum materials respond to light, how electronic order varies across a sample, and how these properties evolve in time.

Cryogenic microscopy

We develop low-temperature scanning probes for local optical and electrical measurements. Earlier work produced a cryogen-free scanning tunneling microscope capable of inelastic tunneling spectroscopy and a cryogenic scattering-type scanning near-field optical microscope (s-SNOM).

  • Cryogen-free scanning tunneling microscopyReview of Scientific Instruments (2016)

Excitons and moiré ferroelectricity

We map exciton energies and linewidths in atomically thin semiconductors. In twisted WSe2, graphene plasmons and local photocurrent reveal ferroelectric domains.

  • Nanoscale exciton spectroscopyNature Communications (2022)
  • Imaging moiré ferroelectricityNature Communications (2023)

Strong light–matter coupling

Strong coupling between light and excitations in solids creates hybrid modes called polaritons. Cryogenic near-field imaging revealed hyperbolic exciton polaritons in the layered magnet CrSBr.

  • Hyperbolic exciton polaritons in CrSBrNature Communications (2023)

Ultrafast dynamics

We aim to use time-resolved near-field microscopy to follow short-lived changes in conductivity, magnetism, and polarization. Related work used terahertz nano-imaging to track plasmon polaritons in graphene.

  • Terahertz nano-imaging of Dirac fluidsScience Advances (2024)
